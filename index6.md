# Security Awareness Training Newsletter

**Project Type:** Security Education / User Awareness / Internal Communications  
**Tools Used:** Canva, Microsoft Sway, Google Workspace, Adobe Illustrator  
**Status:** Ongoing  
**Tags:** `Security Awareness`, `Phishing`, `User Training`, `Digital Hygiene`, `Internal Comms`

---

## Project Overview

This project centers around the creation and delivery of a recurring **Security Awareness Training Newsletter** designed to educate end users on safe digital practices. Each issue dissects real-world phishing examples, offers practical tips on secure behavior, and reinforces the importance of digital citizenship within the organization.

The newsletter serves as an internal campaign to shift cybersecurity from an IT problem to a shared responsibility — using clear, accessible language and actionable advice.

---

## Objectives

- Improve end-user resilience against phishing, scams, and social engineering.
- Encourage secure password habits and responsible use of digital resources.
- Foster a culture of cybersecurity mindfulness across staff and departments.
- Provide quick, digestible education in a non-technical, friendly tone.

---

## Content Strategy

Each issue of the newsletter includes the following recurring sections:

- **Phish Breakdown:** A real or simulated phishing message is dissected with annotations showing red flags (e.g., spoofed sender, urgency, odd URLs).
- **Tip of the Month:** Short, actionable advice around topics like password managers, MFA, or reporting suspicious activity.
- **Did You Know?:** Brief security facts or myths debunked in a user-friendly way.
- **Spotlight Story:** Optional short anecdote involving a real incident (anonymized) to emphasize the human side of security.
- **Call to Action:** Link to report phishing, reset passwords, or engage in a training module.

---

## Creation & Delivery Process

- Designed layouts in **Canva** and **Google Docs** for quick edits and visual clarity.
- Exported and distributed via **email**, **intranet posts**, and **Microsoft Sway** for interactive reading.
- Incorporated **user feedback** and open rates to adjust tone, formatting, and frequency.
- Collaborated with IT Helpdesk and HR teams to align messaging and delivery timing with real-world events (e.g., tax season, summer travel, back-to-school).

---

## Sample Topics Covered

- How to Spot a Phishing Link in Under 10 Seconds
- Why “Password123!” Isn’t as Clever as You Think
- How Cybercriminals Use AI to Sound Like Your Boss
- Should You Click “Unsubscribe”? (Sometimes... no.)
- Secure Your Home Wi-Fi in 3 Easy Steps
- The Real Cost of Clicking the Wrong Link

---

## Results & Impact

- **Open rates consistently over 60%** in a district of 6,000+ staff.
- Increased engagement with phishing simulations: more users reporting emails instead of interacting with them.
- Notable increase in requests for password manager enrollment following relevant newsletter issues.
- Reduced repeat clickers in simulated phishing campaigns.

---

## Lessons Learned

- Effective security communication is about **tone and empathy** as much as it is about facts.
- Users respond best to real-world examples and stories over abstract warnings.
- Design simplicity and accessibility (e.g., alt text, readable fonts) are critical for broad reach.
- Feedback loops matter: reader questions often drive the best future topics.

---

## Future Plans

## Ethical & Accessibility Notes

Content was written with accessibility in mind, adhering to WCAG readability standards wherever possible. All images include descriptive alt text. No personally identifiable information or internal incidents are disclosed without anonymization and permission.
